# Reflections on OSS-NA 2026

<!-- markdownlint-disable-file MD013 MD025 -->

Let me write down some notes from OSSNA. Shengwen also wrote a recap after the event, and his notes cover the talks in much more detail than mine. Most of what I wrote here is just a record of each day's schedule.

- [Open Source Summit North America 2026 recap](https://hackmd.io/@shengwen/ossna2026)
- [OSSNA + ELC 2026 photo album](https://www.flickr.com/photos/linuxfoundation/albums/72177720333089343/)

## May 16 (Saturday)

The last time I went abroad was to Japan with friends. This was only my second trip abroad, and somehow I went straight into a long-haul flight. That day I also stayed up all night rushing the PPT, then only started packing about five hours before leaving. Once I finished packing, it was just in time to head out, and then I went straight to the airport.

I went to check in with my passport, itinerary, and documents. The staff asked whether I had any military service issues. I thought I had not received any draft notice or anything, so probably not? Then I got stopped directly at immigration, which was ridiculous. Luckily I could apply for the permission on the spot. As expected, security was also a mess for me. My laptop is too heavy, and the battery life is not great either, so the whole trip I kept wondering whether I should buy a Mac after going back. But I am really not used to how Macs work or to their keyboards.

After boarding, the three of us basically fell asleep immediately. I finally realized that Jserv does sleep after all, obviously, and he did not seem to sleep as little as I had imagined. It looked like he still got six or seven hours. Sleeping on a plane means waking up on and off the whole time, but I was too tired. Except for getting up a bit during meals, I slept the rest of the time, and then we arrived in San Francisco.

It was 6:30 in the morning when we got to San Francisco. After entering, we first picked up our luggage and sent it through security again, but for some reason we ourselves did not need to go through security again. Once we handed over the luggage, we could go straight in. Since our flight was at 4:20, we sat in the hall for a very long time. During that time I saw the professor sleeping again and again, and only then realized he really sleeps more than I had imagined. I originally thought he was the kind of superhuman who sleeps only three hours a day on average.

Around 11:30, we got ready to take the domestic flight to Minnesota. This was when we went through security again. There were two lanes in the middle of the security checkpoint, which looked like they were only for crowd control, but for some reason the side I picked did not require taking out my laptop. So I had to hurriedly shove it back in again. After security, we ate a simple sandwich, which was extremely expensive at 18 USD. It was cold, but it came with some yellow mustard, so it was actually pretty good. After eating, I saw the professor continue sleeping again, and I woke him up around 4. While waiting, of course, I kept rushing the manuscript.

After boarding the flight to Minnesota, I also took a short nap. I had not slept the day before, so I was really tired. After landing, we met up with senior Zheng Shengwen. Shengwen carried us for the whole trip. Booking the hotel, calling Uber, finding restaurants, looking up places to visit, all of that was handled by him. His English is good, and his Japanese also seems pretty good. Whenever I did not understand something, whether it was conference content or what a server was asking because they spoke too fast, I basically asked Shengwen. He is also very extroverted, can chat with all kinds of people, and is good at throwing out topics. As a very introverted person, I could only be full of admiration.

After meeting up, Shengwen called an Uber to our hotel. This time the four of us stayed in the same room. I do not know how other people's conferences are arranged, but I feel like very few people stay in the same room as their professor. That made us look like we were basically traveling. After getting to the hotel, we revised the talk a bit. Around 2 or 3 in the morning, we went to sleep first.

## May 17 (Sunday)

The next day we woke up around 8. Shengwen found a breakfast place and we all went together. It was a very traditional American breakfast, with scrambled eggs, potatoes, toast, and a meat patty. It tasted pretty great. During this trip I realized that potatoes in the U.S. are all made really well, and almost every meal has potatoes, including but not limited to mashed potatoes, fries, pan-fried potato chunks or patties, and potato chips.

After eating, we went back to the hotel first. I was actually quite nervous on the way back, because at this point I had not even started making the PPT, but the talk was the next day. I had only just finalized the manuscript the day before. Once we got back to the hotel, I immediately started working on the PPT. Since this talk was about the basic architecture, existing tutorials did not really provide a complete diagram, and they also did not explain the full flow underneath after opening an application. So this time my focus was entirely on diagrams for those two parts.

First, I organized the manuscript content into bullet points for the slides. I had Codex help with this part. Codex can generate `.pptx` files directly, which is quite convenient. I asked it to first generate a purely structural PPT with no styling at all, just a plain white background. I probably went back and forth with Codex five or six times, and finally moved the result onto the slide template provided by OSSNA.

The most troublesome part was drawing the diagrams, because at that time I had not drawn any of them yet. I had only left notes in the manuscript about how each diagram should be drawn. So, as you can imagine, I nearly died drawing diagrams that day. It was really from morning to night. The whole process was basically adjusting PPT content, drawing diagrams, adjusting PPT content, drawing diagrams, and repeating that over and over. At the end I cleaned up the manuscript a bit more and pasted it into the notes section of the PPT.

Around 10 that night, I also asked Shengwen to look over the PPT first, because we had left the later introduction and demo for virtio-gpu 3D for him to present on stage. As a result, he started changing many parts while reading it. I was panicking inside and wondering whether this meant I also had to make major changes to my script. Fortunately, I still forced myself through it and finished the revisions. At the time, my PPT was mainly made to assist the script, so if you did not hear what I said, the slides alone would not make sense. Later Shengwen adjusted it into a format where people could understand it just by reading the PPT, since there would be a record and it would be more convenient for others to read afterward.

I do think the later version looked better than the original, but at the time it made me feel completely hopeless. Luckily I still finished around 5 or 6 in the morning, so I guess it ended well.

By the way, dinner that day seemed to be at a Turkish restaurant. I ordered a steak plus bread combo. It tasted pretty good at first, and the sauce was quite rich, but it got a little heavy near the end.

## May 18 (Monday)

Then I slept for about two hours and got up to go to the venue. The venue had breakfast ready. On the first day it was some scones with chocolate chips inside. I liked them quite a lot. I do not know why these are harder to find in Taiwan, and even if you find them, they are expensive, often 70 or 80 NTD each. Breakfast at the venue came with coffee. On the first day I did not know there was milk to add, so I drank black coffee directly. It was extremely bitter.

After breakfast, we went to listen to the Opening. Around 10, we arrived at the room early and rehearsed once. At that point I realized my English was super stuck, and I thought, am I really this bad at English? Fortunately, during the actual talk, I slowed down my speaking speed a little and it became much better. The timing was also about right, which was magical.

The talk itself was not that nerve-racking for me. My previous experience helping teach classes at NCU CS had made me more used to being on stage. For the English part, I barely got by with the script. So the most stressful part of the whole trip was actually rushing the PPT. I was really afraid I would not finish it.

People gradually came in and sat down. There were actually not that few people, maybe more than thirty, filling more than half of the seats. At 11:20, I started on time. The whole process was basically reading from the script and occasionally looking up at the audience. After finishing, I noticed no one asked questions, which was a bit surprising. The professor told me that at a time like this, it is best to find someone you know in the audience to help ask a question. I learned something.

![(The talk room)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/talk-room.png)

After the talk, two people came to chat with us. The first was from Linux Foundation JP, named Hiroyuki Ishii. Later I really wanted to talk with him more. They have a project called Unified HMI, which is a virtio-gpu module for native Linux, and it is built in a client-server form. Before departure, Xu Boyang and I had been discussing whether this form could be used, and then I immediately saw an example here. What a coincidence. The second person worked at Blender. It was a bit of a pity that we did not exchange contact information. Later at the venue, I could also see him chatting with others about rendering-related topics, which was very cool.

![(The talk wrapped up successfully)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/talk-finished.png)

After the talk, it was almost lunchtime, so we went to the venue hall to pick up lunch boxes, which were cold sandwiches with coffee again. After eating, we walked around the booths. I originally wanted to see if there was anything related to game consoles, but there was not. Still, watching Shengwen and the professor chat happily with people there was pretty interesting.

![(The lunch venue)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/lunch-hall.png)

After that, we listened to some other talks. Over the three days, I mostly heard about new things, but I did not really understand how they were actually done. Shengwen would explain them to me, though. I also discovered that it is really hard for me to keep up with some speakers' English accents, so later I decided I would go back and watch the videos with subtitles. During this time there was also a talk by one of the professor's friends about multikernel. The speaker was named Wang Cong, and he radiated kernel expert energy from head to toe. After listening to his talk, we also chatted with him briefly, then went to the next session.

Around after 5, there was a reception, but by then I was already about to fall asleep. When we got to the hall, I found that most people were standing, and there did not seem to be many seats. In the middle of the venue there were different foods you could take yourself, like a buffet. The lines for meat and rice were quite long. After taking a bit of each, I found a corner seat and sat down. After eating, I took out my laptop to organize my schedule and work on a side project a bit, then went outside with Shengwen to walk around and look at the scenery.

![(The professor, Wang Cong, and Mark chatting during the reception)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/reception-chat.jpg)

![(Another reception photo)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/reception-wide-shot.png)

Around after 8, there was a drone show, but it was already drizzling at the time. Later the rain got extremely heavy, so we had to go back indoors and sit for a while. In the end, we took the shuttle back near the venue, walked back to the hotel, took showers, and went straight to sleep around 10:30.

![(The drone show)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/drone-show.jpg)

That night, the professor also helped me write an email to Mr. Hiroyuki Ishii to see if there might be any chance for further exchange, but so far I have not received a reply.

## May 19 (Tuesday)

Waking up this day felt comfortable. Finally, there was a day when I had slept enough. For the previous few weeks in Taipei, I had also been rushing the manuscript and kept feeling sleep-deprived. At last, I got normal sleep here.

The schedule was about the same. We first went to the venue for breakfast. Breakfast that day was muffins, which were also very good. This was also the day I discovered that milk could be added, so breakfast evolved from coffee into coffee with milk. It tasted much better.

This day I heard a talk about debugging the kernel with LLMs. The speaker was from Cloudflare. Internally, they use a skill called kdoc, which can help analyze the causes of kernel panics and even automatically fix them and prepare patches. After listening, I thought it was amazing, but I could not find anything on my phone. Later I realized they had not released it yet and were still going through internal processes. Pain.

Wang Cong also had a talk that day, and it was also related to LLMs. He designed a file system for LLMs called [branchfs](https://github.com/multikernel/branchfs), which lets agents work on multiple patches at the same time and then use a commit-like operation to bring those changes back onto the main files. It looks like it is still being prepared for upstreaming. Since it is also related to my own use case, I went to chat with him a bit.

The professor and Qisheng also had talks that day. After listening to them, Mr. Wang Cong invited us and Mark, another speaker from China, to dinner. We picked a Chinese restaurant, and it was pretty tasty. There was white rice, too. I really did not expect to be this moved by eating white rice outside. Finally, it was not a cold sandwich. Unexpectedly, the Chinese food I ate in the U.S. was all quite good, though it was very salty.

![(Dinner group photo)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/dinner-group.png)

After dinner, we walked around UMN and unexpectedly found the spot where Guoshi had taken his graduation photo.

![(The graduation photo spot)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/guoshi-graduation-photo.png)

Before coming here, I had already thought about doing a cosplay of it. I did not expect to find the place so easily. After taking a few photos, I sent them to the MCL group chat, which was pretty fun. It was still bright here around 8:30 at night, but it would also get bright again around 5 in the morning. That was pretty nice. It felt like daytime became very long. I wonder whether I will suddenly be unused to it after going back to Taiwan.

![(My rough cosplay of the graduation photo)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/graduation-photo-cosplay.png)

After walking around, we went back to the hotel. Then, as usual, I wrote some side project code, took a shower, and went to sleep.

## May 20 (Wednesday)

Breakfast this day suddenly became very strange: energy bars plus bananas. I had been looking forward to seeing what new pastries there would be, so this was a big disappointment. I took two energy bars, thought they were a little too sweet, but still ate them with coffee milk.

During the Opening that day, I saw Linus Torvalds in person. I listened to him chat a bit about his view on AI, which was close to my own understanding. I will probably write a short note about it later, like that previous article, The mind behind Linux.

![(Seeing Linus Torvalds in person for the first time)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/linus-torvalds-keynote.png)

There were originally some topics I really wanted to hear that day, but after checking, almost all of them had English accents I found harder to follow. I thought about it and decided to wander into other sessions instead. In the end I also did not hear anything especially interesting, which was a bit of a pity.

After the day ended, Shengwen said he wanted to walk around Mall of America (MOA). On the way there, we noticed there were more people on the street than usual that day. I do not know why. Maybe it was around 7, right when everyone was getting off work. On the way, we ran into someone who looked like he might be on drugs, yelling at us. After yelling for a while, he even started following behind us and shouting, which scared me to death. Later we walked away quickly and pretended nothing had happened, but thinking back now, it was actually a little dangerous.

When we got to the destination, we originally thought it was a single building, so we searched around there for a long time. Later we realized it referred to that whole street. But by the time we arrived, it seemed like all the shops were closed, so we walked around once and came back. On the way back, I was still afraid we would run into that person again.

Back in the room, I opened my laptop as usual and wrote some code. That day, Guoshi finally replied to me and told me I could go to UMN to buy souvenirs and then visit the art museum, so we decided to buy souvenirs the next day.

For dinner, we ordered Wendy's through Uber Eats and found that it was only about 8 USD. Other meals were usually around 15 or 20 dollars. I do not know whether fast food in the U.S. is really especially cheap. After eating, we rested a bit, then showered and went to sleep.

## May 21 (Thursday)

This day was basically the last day of the conference. It was the RISC-V co-located event, only in the morning, but it required a separate payment and there was no breakfast. I only drank one cup of coffee milk.

That day I heard about a cool thing called SAIL, which seems to be usable for formal verification of RISC-V. Pretty cool.

After the meeting ended, we first had lunch with Mark and listened to him and the professor talk about RISC-V. I also added Mark on WeChat. After that, Shengwen and I went back to UMN again. On Tuesday, we did not know there was a souvenir shop, so we had only walked around briefly and left. This time we stayed longer. Though honestly, we still did not really know where we were, but in any case we found the souvenir shop.

Inside there were some clothes, hats, and things like that, along with little charms and daily goods. In total I bought an umbrella, a water bottle, some charms, two shirts, and two coasters. I thought I could bring a coaster back for Guoshi.

On campus, we also ran into a group of turkeys. There were four of them, and they were huge. When taking photos, I was afraid they might charge over and beat me up, so I kept my distance. Later we asked passersby, and they said these are very common on campus and run around everywhere. So maybe they are kind of like the big silly birds we have back home. Super cool.

![(Huge campus turkeys)](../../../essay/2026-OSS-NA/image/campus-turkeys.png)

After walking around, we found a cafe and sat down. When I went in, I noticed they had green tea, so I ordered a cup. Drinking green tea after such a long time made me happy, and it tasted pretty good, not the very astringent kind. During the time there, I kept listening to Shengwen share investment thoughts, which reminded me of Xian-ge. I feel like I should read some finance stuff in the future too, otherwise I will never have money.

After going back to the hotel, I worked on projects as usual. Shengwen and the others went to lie down first, but by after 11 they still had not gotten up. I also wanted to sleep, so I just showered and went to bed without eating dinner.

## May 22 (Friday)

This whole day was free. We first went to MOA and had a pretty good breakfast. I ordered eggs Benedict, toast, stewed beef, and hash browns. It felt like a standard American breakfast. I really like this kind of American breakfast platter.

Everyone else ordered burgers, and they were huge. I definitely would not have been able to finish one, so luckily I did not follow them. After eating, we called a car and went to the sculpture garden. It was a large park with a huge cherry sculpture in the middle, and various smaller sculptures scattered around. Then we went to MIA, the art museum. At the entrance, we ran into Mr. Hiroyuki Ishii again. I hope he replies later.

The museum was quite large. The second floor had many old pottery pieces, porcelain pieces, sculptures, and things like that, while the third floor was related to oil paintings. Looking at oil paintings, as always, puts me in a good mood. After visiting the museum, we went to a national park to look at a waterfall, then walked around. The river inside seemed to be the Mississippi River. I had only heard of it in geography textbooks before, and now I finally saw the actual thing. While walking, there was also a warning sign at one entrance. It seemed to say there might be wolves, which was a little scary.

After looping around, it was already after 5, so we called a car back to the hotel. Then around after 7, I went out again with Shengwen and the others for dinner.

Since it was the last day, we picked a steakhouse. I ordered a bowl of onion soup and a steak with fries. The onion soup was extremely salty, which was kind of hilarious, but it had a lot of cheese and bread inside, and the onions had absorbed the flavor well. So it was still pretty good, as long as I kept drinking water.

For the steak, they intentionally made the surface a little charred, bringing out some smoky aroma, while the doneness in the middle was just right. It was pretty good. But the sauce on the side was cheese sauce, and honestly I did not understand it. Later I just sprinkled some salt on the steak instead.

After eating, we returned to the hotel. As usual, I wrote a bit of code, organized my luggage, then showered and went to sleep.

## May 23 (Saturday)

Since Shengwen had another conference to attend afterward, he did not leave with us. This time I called the Uber, and only then did I realize how expensive it was. One ride cost 46.74 USD. All I can say is that prices in the U.S. really opened my eyes.

When we got to the airport, we found that the counter staff had not even started work yet. All the counters were empty, so we could not check in, and it seemed like our tickets also could not be checked in online. So we sat in the airport until 11:30 before going over.

After check-in, we went through security and bought a sandwich to eat. This time I deliberately chose a hot one. I really did not want to eat another cold sandwich.

Then we boarded the plane. One cool thing was that this time we sat in the first row, so we could see the flight attendants preparing drinks and things. During the flight, because an older Chinese woman did not understand English, I was even called over to translate. On this domestic flight, they did not provide any proper meals, only some small cookies and drinks. I do not know whether all domestic flights are like this.

After landing, the awkward part was that during the first check-in they had only given us the Sun Country boarding pass, so we still had to go to the EVA Air counter and check in again. But before checking in again, we first had to confirm whether we needed to take our luggage through security again. At around 9, we could finally check in. After finishing, we had to go through security again. For some reason, this time again I did not need to take out my laptop, so I had to hurriedly put it back again.

In the end, it was the usual routine. I slept a bit at the gate, continued sleeping after boarding, and slept almost the whole time except for meals. Then after eating the airplane meal, we arrived in Taiwan, and the trip ended smoothly.

## Reflections and Future Work

The preparation process this time was really too rushed. After all, I had only just finished 2D, and then the topic became 3D. After reviewing it, I think the main reason was that I spent too long writing the development notes. But honestly, if I had not written those first, I probably would not have been able to produce the talk manuscript either. I can only say this kind of thing really takes time.

After this exchange, I got many more ideas. Along with the study group from a while ago, I feel like there may be things that can be combined between the two sides. For example, maybe building a management interface like virt-manager, perhaps cross-platform if possible, or a client-server architecture like Unified HMI. Hopefully in the end it can work together with box64 and Wine to actually run games on a virtual machine.

Also, Marty finally released the code for Kiln recently, and I want to bring it in and try using it. So maybe later I will first make a KVM version in C++ myself, as my own testbench. If I have time, I also want to look at the RISC-V instruction implementation and those parts of box64, assuming I have time.

But all of this has to wait until SMP is done. SMP will greatly affect what the virtual machine architecture should look like, so I need to wait until it is finished before I can align the architectures of our different virtual machines.

Another important takeaway this time was how to package myself. It sounds like LinkedIn needs to be maintained, and I need to keep updating my resume. Maybe the blog should also have English and Japanese versions. I will probably start with this article. But I can already clearly feel that having ability alone is not enough.

Finally, I am really grateful to the professor for providing such a good opportunity and letting us come out like this to exchange with others. This is especially true because I am not even a student at NCKU. I just kept working on the project and somehow got brought along. High-quality exchange itself is a rare thing. This reminds me of when I was writing Cpp Miner before, around 2020, when LLMs were not yet that powerful. The first article I wrote was [value category](https://mes0903.github.io/Cpp-Miner/Miner_main/Value_Categories/). At the time, there was basically no Chinese information about this topic, so I could only slowly search for English blogs and some CppCon recordings.

In the end, it was only after watching [a recording from a study group in Ireland](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=liAnuOfc66o) that I roughly understood what the thing was about. The speaker was named Kris. I wrote emails asking him many questions, really many questions. I bothered him continuously for around two weeks, and he patiently answered all of them one by one. Only then did I finally write the first Cpp Miner article.

For another example, the later [Concept article](https://mes0903.github.io/Cpp-Miner/Miner_main/Concept_SFINAE_DetectionIdiom/) I wrote was also something I completely did not understand when I first saw it. At the time, there was also no Chinese information about it. I kept asking senior Zhang Jiahua about it, listened to his explanations, and he even found implementations for me to look at. Only then did I slowly understand it, and that also took more than a month. Later, when I wrote the [dereference null pointer](https://mes0903.github.io/Cpp-Miner/Miner_BlackMagic/Indirect_through_null_pointer/) article, I also had to read committee papers myself, slowly go through the WG21 lists, and piece together the conclusion about UB bit by bit.

But later, after attending the C++ study group at Aoyi, I realized that there are actually many people who understand these things. You just cannot find the information online. Most people who understand them are too busy and simply do not have time to write documents and share them, or they are not good at writing documents. There are only scattered short blog posts. The content is good, but always not comprehensive enough.

And the things above were only about reading one programming language. Last year, when I discussed things with people from Open Source Dungeon, I also found that nowadays you basically cannot find BSP tutorials in the Chinese community.

During these days following the professor, I found that basically as long as I am willing to put in the time, he will help find many opportunities and share information. For example, when I used to write articles, I would consult him, and he would give suggestions I had never thought of and would even take the initiative to schedule time to discuss. When I asked him about project topics, he could directly throw out topics that matched my needs and were slightly above my ability. This time, while writing and working on things, I suddenly got to spend a week in the U.S. on public funding. I am deeply humbled.

In this process, I often entered projects knowing nothing. The first time I looked at semu's code, I could not even understand the project's Makefile. Then the first time I heard about ACLINT, I did not even know what it was trying to do behind the scenes, and I got scolded in meetings for the first three weeks. This time, when doing virtio-gpu, at the beginning I had never read anything about the graphics stack either.

Although the professor often does not directly give me answers to the questions I ask, and honestly I would prefer that he directly give me answers, he instead throws a pile of materials at me and tells me to read them.

The important point, though, is that I have received so much help even though I am not his student. At the beginning, I only met the professor because I was a research assistant and wrote a [ROS tutorial article](https://mes0903.github.io/ROS/ROS_Tutorial_Introduction/). From then until now, we have basically only interacted online. I think it would be very hard to find a second person willing to provide so much help to an internet stranger who "knows nothing." I am truly grateful to the professor.

Anyway, let's keep going. Karnage said he hopes he can run his Vulkan program on a VM someday. Looks like I still have a lot to do.
